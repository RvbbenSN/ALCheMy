📌 Version Revisions: The Volumetric Unit Discrepancy

Previous iterations assumed that all additive volume columns in the master laboratory workbook were expressed in microlitres. They were not. The discrepancy was uncovered directly within the spreadsheet calculation for V total:

V total = SUMA(Q:AB) + SUMA(AE:AU)/1000

The /1000 divisor applied exclusively to the second block proves that the first block—housing HCl (0,6M), HCl (3M), and HF (3M)—was recorded in millilitres. This aligns with experimental laboratory practice: dilute aqueous acids are dosed in millilitres and function as part of the primary solvent matrix, whereas concentrated acids and neat organic modulators are dosed in microlitres.

The operational impact was critical and silent: dividing these volumes by 1,000 caused the associated water volume to vanish. Consequently, 18% of synthesis records failed volumetric mass balances and were mischaracterised as having unknown solvent composition. Following the correction implemented in src/fix_volume_units_2026_09.py, complete solvent breakdown coverage increased from 81.7% to 99.8% across the high-throughput synthesis subset.

§1. Problem Formulation

The Laboratory Inquiry

Can we predict whether a hypothetical hydrothermal synthesis mixture will yield a crystalline metal-organic framework (MOF), an amorphous precipitate, or an unreacted solution before committing experimental reagents and autoclave time?

Practical Utility

High-throughput screening (HTS) plates accommodate 24 or 96 reaction wells. In exploratory synthesis campaigns, random combinatorial parameter exploration typically yields high failure rates (>70% amorphous or unreacted outcomes). A predictive ranking model enables prioritizing reaction conditions that maximise crystallization probability, effectively filtering out non-viable parameter space.

Pre-Established Success Criteria

Defined prior to model training to prevent post-hoc rationalisation:

  • Baseline Benchmark: Must outperform a stratified baseline and the majority-class naive predictor (predicting the majority incipient class).
  • Evaluation Metric: Macro-averaged F1 score (F1_macro) and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) across 3 classes, ensuring minority crystalline outcomes are not sacrificed for overall accuracy.
  • Minimum Operational Target: F1_macro >= 0.50 under spatially and temporally grouped cross-validation.

§2. Analytic Approach

Why Classification Rather than Regression?

Although powder X-ray diffraction yields continuous numerical descriptors (e.g. amorphous_fraction_blank, n_peaks), crystallisation in exploratory materials chemistry represents a categorical, multi-modal phase phenomenon:

  1. Continuous crystallinity descriptors exhibit non-linear physical regimes and zero-inflation.
  2. The chemist's primary screening decision is discrete: did this condition yield a crystalline framework worth scaling up?
  3. Phase transitions between amorphous glassy gels and crystalline solids involve nucleation barriers, best framed as class boundaries.

The Three Target Classes

  • AMORFO (Amorphous): Featureless diffuse scatter; absence of coherent Bragg reflections; non-crystalline solid or clear solution.
  • INCIPIENTE (Incipient): Poorly crystalline or disordered framework; broad reflections, often coexisting with a pronounced diffuse halo; partial crystallisation.
  • CRISTALINO (Crystalline): Sharp, well-defined Bragg reflections; high peak-to-background ratio; established crystal lattice.

§3. Data Requirements

Predictive features must capture the complete thermodynamic and kinetic state of each synthesis autoclave:

  • Inorganic Precursor Parameters: Metal identity, oxidation state, ionic radius, electronegativity, charge density, and active millimoles.
  • Organic Linker Parameters: Chemical formula, molecular weight, denticity, coordination geometry, and active millimoles.
  • Modulator & Additive Stoichiometry: Active millimoles of pure liquid acids (formic, acetic, propionic, TFA, triflic), organic bases (TEA), and aqueous mineral acids (HCl, HF, KOH).
  • Solvent Composition & Dilution: Primary solvent volumes (water, DMF, DEF, ethanol), total reaction volume, and molar concentrations.
  • Thermal Profile: Heating ramp rate (°C/min), target isothermal temperature (°C), plateau duration (hours), and cooling ramp rate (°C/min).

§4. Data Collection & Lineage

4.1 Data Architecture from Lab Notebook to Machine Learning

Experimental syntheses recorded in multi-tab laboratory workbooks (data/raw/) undergo automated transformation via Notebook 01:

  1. Column standardisation and schema mapping.
  2. Stoichiometric calculations for liquid pure acids and aqueous molar solutions.
  3. Thermal ramp parameter parsing.
  4. Relational UPSERT ingestion into PostgreSQL (synthesis_experiments).
  5. PXRD pattern integration from the multimodal Data Lake (characterization_measurements).
  6. Feature extraction via src/pxrd_descriptors.py into pxrd_descriptors.

4.2 Chemical Featurisation

Raw chemical names are converted into physically meaningful descriptors:

  • Metals: Ionic radius (Shannon), Pauling electronegativity, group, period, valence electrons, hardness (Pearson HSAB).
  • Linkers: Topological descriptors, coordination sites, aromatic ring counts, pKa estimates.

§5. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

5.0 Cohort Selection: Eliminating Confounding Factors

A critical confounding factor was identified in early iterations: mixing conventional single-autoclave syntheses (Teflon-lined steel bombs in ovens) with high-throughput screening plates (HTS multireactor blocks).

  • Conventional syntheses had been run historically to optimise already-known crystalline phases, introducing extreme selection bias.
  • Thermal physics differ fundamentally: microwave and metal heating blocks transfer heat substantially faster than a bulk solvothermal autoclave. Consequently, modelling is restricted strictly to the HTS cohort (synthesis_type = 'HTS').

5.1 SQL Extraction Filters

Filter Condition Rationale
synthesis_type = 'HTS' Homogeneous thermal kinetics and unbiased screening exploration
label_value <> 'SIN_MUESTRA' Excludes empty or unrecovered wells
Exclude ml_include = 'NO' Eliminates unresolvable duplicates and documented metal oxides (§6.3)
Map MEZCLA → CRISTALINO Multi-phase crystalline mixtures confirm framework crystallisation
Exclude linker <> 'Tannic acid' Tannic acid acts as a biological macromolecule violating small-molecule assumptions (§6.4)

5.2 Data Profiling

The dataset comprises 2,172 raw historical synthesis records, filtering down to the homogeneous HTS dataset. Automated profiling checks data types, null fractions, and unique values per feature.

5.3 Missing Values: Imputation vs Chemical Meaning

Missing values in chemical formulations are rarely missing completely at random (MCAR):

  • A null volume for an additive denotes zero added volume, not an unrecorded value. Imputing with column medians would introduce non-existent reagents into syntheses.
  • Structural reagent nulls are filled with physical zero (0.0).

5.4 Class Imbalance

The target distribution within the filtered HTS cohort reveals substantial class imbalance:

  • The majority class is INCIPIENTE (~64% of syntheses).
  • AMORFO comprises ~25%.
  • CRISTALINO comprises only ~11% (167 cases). This 5.8:1 imbalance invalidates standard classification accuracy: a naive model predicting solely INCIPIENTE achieves ~64% accuracy while providing zero predictive utility. Class weighting (class_weight='balanced') is enforced across all algorithms.

5.5 Numerical Feature Distributions

Inspection of continuous features reveals that process parameters are discretised in practice:

  • Temperatures take only nine distinct values across the training set, and two of them account for most of the record.
  • Reaction times take three: 24 h, 48 h and 72 h.
  • Modulator volumes span several orders of magnitude, exhibiting heavy right-tailed skewness.

5.6 Correlation Structures

Cross-correlation analysis demonstrates distinct blocks of highly collinear variables:

  • Metal physical descriptors (radius, atomic weight, electronegativity) form tightly coupled correlation clusters.
  • Solvent fraction ratios exhibit compositional constraint collinearly (summing to unity).

5.7 Bivariate Class Separation

Evaluating individual feature distributions grouped by crystallisation outcome reveals that no single variable linearly separates crystalline from amorphous outcomes.

5.8 Hidden Data Leakage: Grouped Plate Architecture

Syntheses conducted simultaneously within the same multireactor block share identical thermal ramps, identical operator handling, and identical reagent batches. Random cross-validation splits would place syntheses from the same plate into both training and validation folds, leaking batch effects and yielding unrealistically optimistic validation metrics. Validation must be grouped by synthesis plate (GroupKFold on synthesis_plate).

5.9 Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Unsupervised projection across the chemical descriptor space demonstrates significant clustering governed by metal identity rather than continuous reaction conditions.

§6. Data Preparation & ETL

6.1 The Volumetric Discrepancy Resolution

As detailed in the version log, unit standardisation resolved the phantom volume discrepancies, increasing valid solvent breakdown coverage to 99.8%.

6.2 Data Quality Corrections

  1. Standardisation of ligand nomenclature (LIG-A, LIG-B, LIG-C, LIG-D).
  2. Resolution of duplicate sample codes using suffix disambiguation (_v2, _HTS).
  3. Alignment of stoichiometric units to active millimoles (mmol).

6.3 Sample Uniqueness and Insoluble Oxides

Syntheses yielding purely insoluble binary metal oxides (e.g. TiO2, Fe2O3) rather than metal-organic networks were flagged and excluded under ml_include = 'NO', ensuring the model learns coordination framework formation.

6.4 Exclusion of Tannic Acid

Tannic acid represents an extraordinary polyphenol ligand whose polydisperse molecular structure and extreme coordination capacity distort stoichiometric feature spaces. It was removed based on chemical domain consensus.

6.5 Feature Selection & Dimensionality Reduction

Uninformative constants and duplicate tracking columns were pruned, leaving a curated set of chemical, stoichiometric and thermodynamic predictors.

6.6 Imputation Strategy

Deterministic zero-filling for unadded reagents; iterative k-NN imputation for minor missing physical descriptors, verified against known elemental tables.

§7. Predictive Modelling

7.1 Experimental Design

Six algorithmic families spanning different inductive biases were benchmarked:

  1. Dummy Baseline Classifier: Stratified and majority-class prediction.
  2. Logistic Regression (L2 regularised): Linear baseline establishing linear separability.
  3. Gaussian Naive Bayes: Probabilistic baseline assuming conditional independence.
  4. Random Forest: Non-linear ensemble insensitive to monotonic feature scaling.
  5. Extra Trees Classifier: Randomised decision trees with enhanced variance reduction.
  6. Gradient Boosted Trees (LightGBM / HistGradientBoosting): Sequentially boosted shallow trees optimising multinomial log-loss.

7.2 Grouped Cross-Validation Protocol

To honour the grouped plate structure demonstrated in §5.8, all models are evaluated via GroupKFold cross-validation (5 folds) grouped strictly on synthesis_plate. No synthesis condition from a validation plate ever appears within the corresponding training fold.

7.3 Dual Formulation: Full Model vs Actionable Model

Two distinct feature formulations are evaluated:

  • Full Model (39 features): Incorporates all metal descriptors, linker parameters, solvent ratios, modulators, and process conditions. Evaluates total learnable signal.
  • Actionable Model (Process & Formulation Only): Excludes metal and linker intrinsic descriptors, restricting inputs strictly to laboratory variables a chemist can tune for an already-selected metal-linker pairing (additive volumes, solvent fractions, temperatures, reaction times).

§8. Performance Evaluation

Stage 8 — Evaluation. Not merely "what metric was achieved?" but "is this metric trustworthy, and what does it convey to experimental chemists?".

8.1 Performance Metrics

Metric Measurement Scope Methodological Role
Macro F1 (F1_macro) Unweighted mean of class F1 scores Primary metric. Heavily penalises neglecting the 167 crystalline cases
Balanced Accuracy Mean recall evaluated within each class Interpretable: "correctly identifies X% of true crystalline phases"
MCC Multi-class Matthews Correlation Coefficient (−1 to +1) Rigorous chance-adjusted correlation: 0 indicates random performance
Accuracy Fraction of total correct predictions Diagnostic warning only: a naive majority predictor achieves ~64%

Reminder from §2: MRE, RMSE, and R² do not apply—they evaluate regression tasks.

8.2 Analysis of Empirical Results

1. Genuine predictive signal exists, but it is modest. The leading full model achieves F1_macro ≈ 0.52, compared to 0.26 for the dummy baseline: an exact two-fold improvement. The associated MCC evaluates to ≈ 0.31, confirming a statistically authentic yet moderate correlation.

2. Correcting the volumetric units did NOT improve model performance, and this must be stated transparently. The previous iteration, where 18% of synthesis solvent compositions were flagged as unknown, achieved an F1_macro of 0.522. The corrected dataset, boasting 99.8% solvent completeness, evaluated to 0.517. The difference of 0.005 is seven times smaller than cross-validation standard deviation (±0.036): statistically, the models are identical.

This represents an uncomfortable and honest scientific finding. Completing solvent composition for an entire fifth of the dataset conferred zero additional predictive power. Two interpretations exist, neither of which can be definitively resolved from current data: either solvent formulation does not govern crystallisation to the extent chemical intuition assumes, or it does govern it via mechanisms not captured by linear volume fractions (such as solution pH, which was not experimentally recorded). What was gained is that the numbers genuinely represent what they claim to represent, which is a prerequisite for chemical interpretability.

3. The actionable model experiences substantial degradation. Omitting metal and linker intrinsic descriptors causes performance to drop to 0.393 with MCC ≈ 0.10—exceeding random chance, but weak.

Unvarnished chemical interpretation: within this historical dataset, the vast majority of what the machine learning model learns is the identity of the metal-linker system, rather than the applied synthesis conditions. This does not imply that synthesis conditions are irrelevant. Rather, it demonstrates that within this historical dataset, conditions were not varied with sufficient breadth to render their effects learnable (§5.5: ~13 temperatures, ~9 reaction times).

4. Naive Bayes collapses below the random baseline. This is not an implementation defect: Naive Bayes assumes features are conditionally independent given the class, whereas §5.6 demonstrated pronounced correlation blocks. Its failure quantifies the critical importance of feature covariance.

8.3 Error Analysis: Chemical Plausibility of Misclassifications

The critical consideration is not merely how frequently the model fails, but the directionality of its errors. The ordinal crystallisation pattern is cleanly observed in one direction, but only partially in the other.

For amorphous syntheses, behaviour aligns with physical intuition: misclassifications predominantly confound "amorphous" with "incipient"—a boundary that in experimental diffraction patterns is a matter of degree, where two expert crystallographers might disagree. Severe errors (classifying an amorphous outcome as crystalline) are rare.

For crystalline syntheses, the behaviour is less forgiving. While most errors assign crystalline cases to the neighbouring incipient class, the fraction misclassified as amorphous—the most severe failure mode—is double that of the reverse error (15.6% vs 7.9%). This indicates that the model struggles to generalise across the minority crystalline class, and when it fails, it fails completely. With only 167 crystalline cases against 967 incipient records, and given that crystalline outcomes exhibit diverse polymorphic forms, this limitation was anticipated; it remains the primary weakness of the model.

Operational consequences: For Stage 9—ranking synthesis candidates to populate a 24-well multireactor plate—this limitation is manageable: the requirement is that promising candidates rank higher in relative probability, rather than assigning perfect absolute probabilities. However, it refutes any claim that "the model almost never commits severe errors".

8.4 Chemical Interpretability: Feature Blocks vs Isolated Variables

Why evaluate feature blocks? With highly correlated descriptors (§5.6), attribution algorithms distribute importance arbitrarily among collinear inputs. Interpreting electronegativity_pauling in isolation from absolute_hardness_ev would represent over-interpretation.

Evaluating importance by chemical family (aggregating permutation feature importance):

  • Metal chemistry dominates completely, both in aggregate and per variable (sum of 0.071 across 7 features). Dominant drivers are Shannon ionic radius and charge density, governing the electrostatic strength of metal-phosphonate coordination. This represents a direct physical mechanism rather than an algorithmic artifact.
  • Process parameters rank second, nearly matching metal chemistry on a per-variable basis (sum of 0.039 across only 4 features: 0.0099 each vs 0.0101 for metals). Temperature and duration exert significant leverage, constrained solely by their narrow historical sampling range (§5.5).
  • Linker descriptors rank lower (sum of 0.028 across 6 features, 0.0046 per variable): with only 4 distinct linkers in the dataset, there is limited variability to learn.
  • Solvent formulation is by far the weakest predictor per variable (0.0011 per column across 22 features, one-ninth the influence of metals), despite being fully corrected and validated in §6.1. Repairing the data did not transform it into predictive signal. In fact, 12 of the 22 solvent variables exhibit negative permutation importance: permuting them slightly improves validation performance, which is the operational definition of noise.

Chemical context. That solvent fractions contribute minimal importance does not prove they are chemically inactive. Rather, it indicates that these 22 composition fractions, under this sample size and model architecture, fail to separate outcomes. What genuinely governs crystallisation kinetics is likely in-situ pH, which was not historically recorded.

Interpreting the SHAP Beeswarm Plot: Each point represents a synthesis. Horizontal displacement reflects the log-odds push toward the CRISTALINO class. High values (red) located to the right indicate that elevated values of that feature favour crystallisation.

§9. Model Deployment & Registry

9.1 Model Serialisation

Trained pipelines (preprocessing, imputer, feature selectors, and calibrated classifier) are exported using joblib into models/. Serialising full pipelines prevents train-test feature misalignment during inference.

9.2 Model Registry Architecture

Model metadata is tracked in PostgreSQL within a dedicated registry table:

  • Hyperparameters, feature schema versions, cross-validation metrics (F1_macro, MCC, balanced accuracy).
  • Training git commit hash, data snapshot MD5 checksum, and training timestamp.

9.3 Deployment Pipeline

The deployed model is exposed via the n8n orchestration platform and local CLI utilities (src/predict_synthesis.py), accepting hypothetical stoichiometry and outputting ranked crystallisation probabilities for high-throughput screening planning.

§10. Feedback Loop & Synthesis Optimisation

Delivered Capabilities

  • A robust, validated classification pipeline achieving F1_macro ≈ 0.52 (2x over baseline).
  • Rigorous quantification showing that metal charge density and thermal kinetics govern historical crystallisation.
  • Transparent demonstration that solvent volume fractions alone provide weak predictive signal without pH tracking.

Recommendations for the Experimental Laboratory

  1. Systematically vary reaction temperatures and dwell times: Historic clustering on two of the nine temperatures used suppresses process-parameter leverage.
  2. Implement in-situ or pre-reaction pH tracking: The 22 solvent features act as a proxy for acid-base equilibria; recording pH directly will likely recover the missing chemical signal.
  3. Target active exploration for minority metals: Expanding exploration around intermediate charge-density cations will improve class boundary definition.

The Essential Conclusion

Data science applied to experimental chemistry does not replace chemical insight: it highlights where historical experimentation suffered from unconscious human bias, and clarifies which physical parameters must be rigorously recorded moving forward.
