⚗️ Step 3: Stoichiometric Engineering for Modulators & Solvents

🧪 Stoichiometric Calculations & Mathematical Formulas:

Unit correction (2026-09-03): mL versus µL. This notebook previously assumed that all additive columns were recorded in microlitres. They were not. The evidence lies within the formula of the V total column in the original spreadsheet:

V total = SUMA(Q:AB) + SUMA(AE:AU)/1000

This /1000 divisor is applied exclusively to the second block, demonstrating that the first block—containing HCl (0,6M), HCl (3M), and HF (3M)—is expressed in millilitres. This aligns with laboratory practice: these are dilute solutions, dispensed in mL and acting as part of the solvent system; concentrated solutions and neat liquids are dosed in µL.

The consequence was severe and silent: dividing these volumes by 1,000 caused the water they contributed to vanish. Total volumes failed to balance in 18% of the syntheses, leaving those rows flagged with unknown solvent composition. No solvent was missing; rather, the dilution water had been omitted. Following this correction, complete solvent breakdown coverage increased from 81.7% to 99.5% of rows.

The database has already been corrected using src/fix_volume_units_2026_09.py, which updates only the affected columns. This notebook has been amended to prevent future re-ingestions from reintroducing the error, but it is not re-executed: a full ingestion run would overwrite manual chemical corrections stored in the database.

1. Type A: Molar Aqueous Solutions (Acid/Base Solutions)

Conversion of dispensed volumes to active millimoles. Unit consistency is critical: dilute solutions are provided in mL and concentrated solutions in µL:

mmol_HCl = V_0.6M(mL) * 0.6 + V_1M(mL) * 1.0 + V_3M(mL) * 3.0 + V_5M(mL) * 5.0 + V_12M(mL) * 12.0
mmol_HF = V_3M(mL) * 3.0
mmol_KOH = V_10M(mL) * 10.0

2. Type B: Pure Organic Liquid Acids & Modulators

Calculation of active millimoles (mmol) directly from dispensed volume V (µL), density ρ (g/mL), purity percentage P_%, and molecular weight MW (g/mol):

mmol = [V(µL) * ρ(g/mL) * P_%] / [100 * MW(g/mol)]

Unit correction (2026-08-29). This formula previously contained a factor of * 10 / MW instead of / (100 * MW): an overestimate by a factor of 1,000. Consequently, the seven neat liquid modulators were computed in µmol while labelled as mmol, whereas the Type A entries (HCl, HF, KOH, derived from molar solutions) were calculated correctly.

The issue was not merely the scaling factor itself—a uniform linear transformation does not disrupt model behaviour—but that both families are summed together in v_ml_features. Conflating mmol with µmol caused HCl (0.004) to be dwarfed by TFA (582), effectively rendering the most frequently used modulator in the dataset invisible to machine learning models.

The discrepancy was uncovered through domain-specific chemical scrutiny: the median dosage of formic acid evaluated to 1,168 'mmol', which corresponds to 54 g in a 2.5 mL reactor. No standard statistical metric had flagged the anomaly.
